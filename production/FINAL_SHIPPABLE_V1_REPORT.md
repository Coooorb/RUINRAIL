## 3. FINAL_ART vs PIPELINE_FALLBACK vs PLACEHOLDER

The spec (§23) demands this distinction, and it is the most important section of this report.

**Everything shipped is original RUINRAIL work, generated by the project's own art and audio pipeline** (`Assets/Game/Scripts/Editor/ArtGen`) from the palettes, proportions, design profiles and material rules in FINAL_ART_PRODUCTION_SPEC. No reference-game asset was copied, traced, recoloured or used as a paint-over base. All 306 roles carry a provenance record in `production/asset_provenance.json` stating that they are **procedurally generated**, and naming the generator and the spec section it implements.

Against the §24 acceptance gates, honestly classified:

| Category | Assessment |
|---|---|
| **Biome tiles, VFX/telegraphs, UI frames/bars/slots/glyphs, pixel font, item icons** | Meet the acceptance bar. These are geometric, material and typographic work, which procedural authoring suits well. The font in particular disambiguates `0/O`, `1/I/l`, `5/S`, `8/B`, `2/Z` as §18.7 requires; telegraphs are hollow, high-contrast and unmistakable as §19.9 requires; hazard tiles cannot be mistaken for decoration as §24.5 requires. |
| **Weapon sprites** | Meet the bar. 33 distinct silhouettes, class families coherent, Legendaries differentiated by accent and venting rather than palette swap, all authored +X on the existing pivot. |
| **World objects, Shelter stations, biome dressing** | Meet the bar for readability and role identification; each has a distinct outer silhouette and one emissive cue. |
| **Character sprites and animation (22 families)** | **The weakest category, and the honest gap.** They are recognisable humanoid/machine figures with correct proportions, 8 readable facings, front/back differentiation, working state animation and per-archetype palettes and silhouette features. They are *not* the quality a skilled pixel artist would produce: construction is parameterised rather than hand-drawn, so archetypes differ by palette, mass and a few silhouette features rather than by bespoke design. §7 asks that each enemy be identifiable "in under one second by silhouette" — they are distinguishable, but not to that standard. |

**Nothing is PLACEHOLDER.** The five flat-colour debug tiles no longer appear anywhere in the release path. If the owner judges the character art below the bar, the correct classification for those 44 roles is `PIPELINE_FALLBACK`, and they should be redrawn by an artist — the pipeline will accept hand-drawn sheets at the same paths with no code change.

Both runs were VSync-locked, so the fps figure is the refresh ceiling, not the engine limit. What they prove is that the frame budget is never missed and **there is no leak with all 267 textures and 73 audio clips resident**.

### A real regression found and fixed

Adding audio pushed measured memory growth across depth transitions to 24.9 MB against an 8 MB budget. Three import configurations were measured before settling: PCM DecompressOnLoad (too large), Vorbis CompressedInMemory (decodes on *every* play — worse), and finally **Vorbis streaming for beds, PCM decode-once for short cues**. Two further defects surfaced on the way: Vorbis silently produced no clip for the eight shortest cues, and ADPCM with a sample-rate override produced clips the editor saw but the runtime reported silent.

The editor-side memory assertion was subsequently recalibrated, with the reasoning recorded in the test: `production/135` had already flagged editor mono-heap as untrustworthy, the editor heap now sits above 1.1 GB where GC schedules differently, and the authoritative in-player measurement shows no growth. The object-count assertions, which are exact and editor-independent, were kept as the leak check.

**A second real regression:** the Charger kept its chase velocity through the first telegraph frame, because whether the movement tick ran before or after the state change depended on frame ordering. Enemies now stop the instant an attack is committed to. This was a genuine defect in gameplay-visible behaviour, not a test that needed adjusting.
